What is "Your Life in Weeks"?

If you live to 90, you get about 4,680 weeks. That's the entire thing — childhood, every birthday, every Monday, every summer. Laid out as a field of stars, a human life fits on a single screen.

The life in weeks calendar — sometimes called a life calendar or memento mori calendar — turns the abstract idea of "time" into something you can actually see. Instead of vague decades, you get a finite, countable constellation. The weeks you've already lived are lit. The rest are dark: your future, still unwritten.

The idea was popularized by Tim Urban's essay "Your Life in Weeks," and it has resonated with millions of people for one simple reason — it makes the value of a single week impossible to ignore.

How to read your life calendar

Once you enter your birthday above, the constellation ignites row by row. Here's what you're looking at:

The lit stars

Each glowing star is a week you have already lived, colored by the stage of life it belongs to — the blues of childhood, the violets of your prime, the warm tones of later years. Together they show how far into your life you currently are.

The brightest star

One pure-white star marks the week you're living right now. This is the only week you actually control. Everything before it is memory; everything after is possibility.

The dark stars

These are the weeks you have left, based on the life expectancy you chose. Not a prediction — a canvas waiting to be lit.

How many weeks are in a life?

It depends on the lifespan you assume. A 90-year life is roughly 4,680 weeks (90 × 52). An 80-year life is about 4,160 weeks. A 75-year life is around 3,900. Adjust the life expectancy field above to match your own outlook — the constellation recalculates instantly.

Why people find this motivating, not depressing

At first glance a memento mori calendar can seem grim. In practice, most people find it does the opposite. Seeing your weeks laid out sharpens your priorities — it gets harder to spend a whole star, a whole week of your one life, on things that don't matter to you. It's less "you're running out of time" and more "this week actually counts."

Frequently asked questions

What is a life in weeks calendar?

It's a visualization of your entire lifespan as a grid where each point represents one week. A typical 90-year life is about 4,680 weeks. Lighting up the weeks you've lived gives a tangible sense of how time passes.

How do you calculate how many weeks you've lived?

Count the days between your birth date and today, then divide by seven. Someone exactly 30 years old has lived roughly 1,560 weeks. This tool does the math automatically.

Is the life in weeks idea morbid?

Most people find it clarifying rather than morbid. Seeing your time as finite tends to be motivating — it helps you prioritize what matters and stop postponing the things you care about.
